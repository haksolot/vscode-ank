`"contract": 1` leads every `--json` document. It is not the version of the
binary: `ank --version` says which build is in hand, this says which shapes
came out of it.

Within one version a document may **gain** a field, and may never lose, rename
or retype one. Both halves of that promise have a consequence here:

- A parser that refuses an unknown field turns a compatible ank release into a
  broken extension. So every type is read positively — take the fields we know,
  ignore the rest.
- A change of the contract number is the signal to look again. The extension
  reports it once, plainly, and degrades to a read-only view rather than
  guessing at shapes it was not written against.

`nullable` is separate from the type and is load-bearing. `null` and `""` are
different answers: `status.branch` is null on a detached HEAD, `status.refs` is
null unless `--remote` was passed and null is not zero. Every nullable field is
typed `T | null` and never `T | undefined`.
