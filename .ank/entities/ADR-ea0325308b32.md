`accept` turns a proposed decision into a binding one through a signed
ratification commit, on the default branch, with no way around it. It is the
one hard authority line in a tool that otherwise refuses on state and never on
who is asking.

The skill says it plainly: *accept is not yours to run.* An extension that ran
it from a button would be signing on the user's behalf from a click that looked
like every other click in the tree.

Composing the line and stopping is not a lesser feature. It is the same move
`ank tui` makes for every write it offers — nothing runs until the command line
it composed is on the screen and confirmed.
